## Introduction

The field of simulation is theoretically larger than the contents of the universe! To narrow down our subject, this semester, DIG320 is only Skin Deep! That is, we will focus almost entirely on simulating surfaces  
That's still a wide field of inquiry so we'll follow a small handful of...

## Searchlights:

* **Textured Surfaces**   
We're probably all familiar with image textures which can make a plain cube or other primitive geometry take on the appearance or brick, stone, ice or etc. and we'll do this kind of texturing  
But there are other more dynamic textures we can use and we'll explore some of them as well

* **Lit Surfaces**  
In modeling complex topography for interactive applications, whether the skin of an Orc or a planet's surface, we cannot always afford the millions of polygons which would give a high level of detail to such simulations  
To work within real-time computational limits is is often enough to apply photographic texture maps, height or bump maps and other supplementary maps which will, among other things, tell the virtual lights in a scene how to scatter, shade or interpret our topographies as if they contained much more detail than the low-poly model actually presents   

* **Responsive Surfaces**  
Materials that morph, bend, flow can be simulated in a number of ways, from ragdoll physics to shaders  
This searchlight will illuminate some of the ways we can give our materials the sense of natural aliveness we observe in the real world of fire, water, mud, etc.

## Learning Goals and Objectives

This course will steer by these searchlights, toward these destinations:

### Goals

* Design surfaces and materials that simulate reality   
* Build up layers of simulation effects which create a sense of immersion in a habitable space
* Add interactivity to simulation, allowing users to play with and alter the simulation

### Objectives 

* **Cost Benefit analysis:** Learners will develop techniques that approach reality in environments where computation overhead is at a premium
* **Deep Understanding:** They will develop the ability to simulate at greater or lesser granularity, depending upon the application 
* **Theoretical Context:** They will project out to future applications, following Moore's law, to predict what will be possible at certain dates at which an application is needed, and design for the capacities they anticipate to come on line at that time

## Computing Requirements 

Besides Adobe Photoshop (and possibly Illustrator and After Effects) which should all be installed in the classroom computers, we'll be using three main softwares:

* **Autodesk Maya** —an Industry Standard tool, free to students, which we'll use for 3D modeling, texturing, lighting, etc

* **Unity** —The unity game engine is the target for our simulated surfaces, and free to students

> Though all the above softwares should be installed on classroom computers, students are urged to download and install free student versions where available
